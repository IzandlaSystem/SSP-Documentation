Device & companion app guide

Getting the most out of your SSP-S1

The SSP-S1 is a wearable performance tracker: it records motion and GPS during training or matches, then hands that data to the SSP Mobile App over Bluetooth for analysis. This guide covers pairing, what the status light is telling you, how sessions start and stop, and how to find your way around the app.

For coaches & players Android & iOS SSP-S1 tracker, all firmware
01

Pairing the tracker

Do this once per tracker. After it's paired, the app reconnects to it automatically whenever it's nearby and powered on.

  1. Turn on the tracker and open the SSP app. From the Devices tab, tap Add device.
  2. Wait for the scan. The app lists nearby SSP trackers by signal strength. If nothing appears, make sure the tracker is powered on and that Bluetooth and Location are enabled on your phone: the app needs Location permission to scan for Bluetooth devices, which is an Android requirement, not something the tracker itself uses.
  3. Select your tracker from the list, then give it a name you'll recognise later (e.g. a player's name or a squad number).
  4. Confirm. Once paired, the tracker appears on your Devices screen and the app will connect to it automatically from now on, any time it's powered on and in range.
Good to know

One phone can be paired with several trackers, and the app remembers each one. You only need to repeat this if you're setting up a tracker for the first time or re-pairing after an unpair.

02

The status light

The tracker has one status light. Its colour and pattern tell you what the device is doing right now. No app required.

LightMeaning
Off Powered off.
Red, blinking Internal fault: the tracker's two internal processors have lost contact with each other. Power-cycle the tracker; contact support if it keeps happening.
Blue, blinking On, standing by, searching for a GPS fix. Not recording yet.
Blue, steady On, standing by, GPS fix acquired. Ready to record.
Green, steady Recording a session, still waiting on a GPS fix. Motion is being captured regardless.
Cyan, steady Recording a session, GPS fix acquired. This is the normal state during outdoor training.
Common mix-up

The status light does not show whether your phone is connected: it only reflects GPS and recording state. A tracker can be blinking blue (standing by) whether or not any phone is nearby. To check the connection, look at the app's Devices screen instead.

You may see red during a sync

The red light is the tracker's internal "lost contact" signal between its two processors, normally caused by the two halves being out of touch. Under a large or slow sync (a lot of session data transferring over a weak connection), that same signal can flash briefly, because the internal link watchdog runs on a tight budget while it's busy moving data.

Treat a brief flash or two during an active sync as normal: it should clear on its own once the transfer catches up or finishes. If the light stays solid red for more than a few seconds, or shows up outside of a sync, treat it as the fault case above and power-cycle the tracker.

03

Starting & stopping a session

A session can start two ways: you can trigger it yourself, or the tracker can notice activity on its own.

Manually

Press the tracker's button once. A short press toggles recording: it starts a session if the tracker was idle, and stops the current one if it was already recording. You can also start or stop from the app's Devices screen.

Automatically

If the tracker isn't recording and detects around a minute of sustained movement, it starts a session on its own, no button press needed. That way a warm-up or drill that starts before anyone reaches for the app still gets captured. It's deliberately unhurried: brief handling or picking the unit up won't trigger it.

An auto-started session ends the same way, automatically, after about three minutes without meaningful movement. A session you started manually never ends on its own; it keeps recording through breaks and huddles until you stop it yourself.

Taking over an auto session

If the tracker auto-starts during your warm-up and you then press the button, rather than starting a second session, it adopts the one already running as yours. That session keeps its warm-up data and, from that point on, behaves like a manual session: it won't cut out after three minutes of standing around.

One more detail: after any session ends by button press or from the app, auto-start stays paused for five minutes. Otherwise, deliberately stopping a session while still moving around would have the tracker start a new one moments later.

04

What gets saved, and when

You don't need to do anything to save a session: it's written continuously while recording, then handed to the app in the background.

On the tracker

Data is written to the tracker's internal memory the moment a session starts, and saved incrementally throughout, not just at the end. If the tracker loses power mid-session, at most a few seconds of data are lost, never the whole recording. The session is marked complete the moment you (or auto-stop) end it.

Getting to your phone

Whenever your phone is running the app and connected to the tracker, new sessions are found and downloaded automatically in the background: you don't need to open a specific screen or tap anything. A Sync now button on the Devices screen is there if you want to force a check. Sessions already on your phone stay available even when the tracker's out of range.

Why a session might not show up

Only sessions with meaningful activity (roughly 100 steps or more) appear in your session history. Very brief or accidental recordings (bumping the button, a few seconds of auto-detect) are still kept safely on the tracker, they don't clutter your list. This is a display filter, not data loss.

Full analytics can take up to a day

A session shows up in your app within moments of syncing, but its Training Load, intensity, and route charts need one more step: the server turns the raw recording into those numbers on a scheduled job that currently runs once every 24 hours. Until that job runs, the session sits fine and complete, just waiting to be processed, so a session synced this morning may not show full numbers until tomorrow's run. Nothing is lost while it waits.

If you need a specific session's numbers sooner, ask us and we can trigger that processing manually. It finishes in under a minute once triggered.

Phase 2: faster processing

The once-a-day limit comes from the hosting plan the server currently runs on, not from anything about the tracker or the app itself. The plan for phase 2 is to move that processing job somewhere without that restriction, either by upgrading the current hosting plan or by moving the job to a different host, so sessions get their full analytics within minutes instead of up to a day.

05

Finding your way around the app

Four tabs, reached from the floating bar at the bottom of the screen. What you see depends on whether you're signed in as a player or a coach.

Home

Your weekly performance summary, tracker readiness at a glance, and (players) goal progress.

Analytics

Training load, intensity, and distance charts. Pick any day, week, or month, and tap a chart point to zoom in on that session's data.

Trainer players / Squad coaches

Today's session focus for players; the full roster and team overview for coaches.

Profile

Account details, app settings, and sign-out.

Devices

Reached from Home or the header: this is where pairing, connection status, and your recorded sessions live. While connected, it also shows a live map of the tracker's current GPS position instead of raw sensor numbers. Sessions are listed newest-first, numbered sequentially (Session 1, Session 2, …) rather than by an internal device ID, with a short place name once it's been worked out from the route.

Zooming into a session's data

On the Analytics tab, tapping a point on any chart narrows the view: from month to a specific day, then from that day into the hour, minute, and finally second the activity happened in. A Zoom out button appears once you've zoomed in, and steps back out one level at a time. This re-fetches the finer-grained data each time instead of visually stretching the chart.
